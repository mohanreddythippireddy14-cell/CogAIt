# CogAIt: Think First, AI Next

## The problem: AI is outsourcing our thinking
When students rely on AI for instant answers, they skip the cognitive effort that builds real understanding. Routine use of AI for solutions and step-by-step walkthroughs feels productive, but it replaces the mental struggle required for deep learning. The result is a "reasoning gap": students recognise patterns but cannot apply first principles under exam pressure.

## The solution
CogAIt is a Socratic AI tutor for JEE and NEET aspirants that is designed **not** to hand out answers. It strengthens reasoning by keeping the student doing the work.

### The 4-level help framework
Every reply is a question or a small nudge. The level of help rises only when the student is stuck:
1. **Audit**: one open conceptual question; no method or formula.
2. **Socratic**: acknowledges the student's thinking and exposes a gap through a question.
3. **Instruction**: may name a relevant concept or formula, framed as a question about how to apply it.
4. **Deep assistance**: one concrete next step or micro-hint; the student still does every calculation.

Replies are checked before the student sees them. Final numerical answers, step-by-step solutions and direct "that's correct" confirmations are rejected and regenerated, with a fixed fallback question if needed.

## Why it matters
- **Earn, don't get.** Most AI study tools help students *get* answers. CogAIt helps them *earn* them.
- **Visibility for teachers.** Each answer gets a Cognitive Independence Score (CIS). Lecturer dashboards show per-topic dependency and flag students who lean on AI.
- **Analysis after the session.** A set of small agent services turns submissions into weak-topic signals, remediation offers, long-term student reports, cohort patterns and lecturer briefings.

- **JEE and NEET aspirants**, preparing for exams that test thinking under uncertainty, not recall.
- **Teachers and institutes** that want class-level insight into reasoning and AI dependency.
